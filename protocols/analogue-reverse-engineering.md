# Analogue reverse-engineering protocol

Every repository-genesis candidate must be compared with at least three analogue classes.

A direct analogue addresses substantially the same user and problem. An adjacent analogue uses a transferable mechanism in another domain. A failed or constrained analogue reveals adoption, policy, economics, timing, trust, data, workflow, or distribution limits.

For each analogue reconstruct the initiating conditions, actor map, value exchange, enabling infrastructure, distribution path, proof of demand, operating model, governance, failure mode, and present outcome. Separate copied form from transferable mechanism. Extract only mechanisms supported across multiple examples.

The synthesis must state what repeats, what depends on context, what failed, what the candidate deliberately changes, and which lesson is unsafe to transfer. A candidate fails this protocol when it relies on one celebrated example, ignores failed examples, or confuses similar branding with similar operating structure.
