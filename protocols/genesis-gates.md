# Repository genesis gates

## G0 — Source integrity

Every load-bearing claim has provenance, retrieval time, source class, and uncertainty. Failure is fatal.

## G1 — Problem reality

The problem is demonstrated through observable behavior rather than opinion alone. At least two actor classes must show relevant movement.

## G2 — Convergent evidence

At least six evidence dimensions are independently represented: market, policy, research, innovation, economic, traction, ecosystem fit, technical feasibility, funding, or community demand. Correlated reposts count once.

## G3 — Socratic integrity

The problem, beneficiary, mechanism, dangerous assumption, falsifier, missing evidence, and simpler alternative explanation are explicit.

## G4 — Analogue reconstruction

At least one direct, one adjacent, and one failed or constrained analogue have been reverse-engineered.

## G5 — Existing-system fit

The proposal proves why it is not merely a feature, package, plugin, notebook, service, case study, research note, or cross-repository initiative. Failure routes the work to the appropriate existing container.

## G6 — Smallest proof

A bounded experiment targets the most dangerous assumption with a measurable pass, fail, and stop condition.

## G7 — Strategic coherence

The proposal strengthens the portfolio and ecosystem without creating avoidable fragmentation, duplicated primitives, or an unsupported maintenance burden.

## G8 — Governance

Confidence is at least 0.82, no fatal gate has failed, and explicit human approval is recorded before repository creation.

## G9 — Birth and measurement

An approved repository begins with a thesis, boundaries, first milestone, acceptance tests, owner, license decision, rollback or archive condition, and measurement plan. Genesis is treated as an executable hypothesis, not proof of success.
