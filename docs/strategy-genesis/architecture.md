# Strategy and Repository Genesis Engine v0

## Purpose

This subsystem converts verified external signals and internal repository evidence into bounded strategic recommendations, then determines whether an opportunity should be absorbed by an existing repository, tested as an experiment, or proposed as a new repository.

It is not an autonomous venture studio. It may observe, compare, infer, challenge, rank, and propose. It may not merge code, create a production repository, accept knowledge, commit funds, represent market certainty, or alter governance without explicit human approval.

## System position

The engine sits above the living research graph and below human governance.

Research collectors produce source records. The provenance layer normalizes and hashes evidence. The observation layer separates verified fact from inference. The pattern layer identifies recurring mechanisms. The strategy layer explains what convergence means for this ecosystem. The opportunity layer tests whether timing, demand, capability, policy, funding, and differentiation reinforce one another. The genesis layer proposes a repository thesis only when existing repositories cannot contain the work cleanly. The adversarial layer attempts to disprove the thesis. The experiment layer tests the most dangerous assumption. Human governance authorizes, defers, narrows, redirects, or rejects the proposal. Implementation produces evidence that returns to the graph.

## Roles

The Collector finds evidence but does not interpret it. The Verifier checks dates, source identity, primary-versus-secondary status, and contradiction. The Observer states what changed. The Pattern Synthesizer identifies repeatable structure across domains. The Strategist explains benefit, cost of delay, leverage, timing, and likely strategic posture. The Ecosystem Mapper evaluates fit with existing repositories and identifies reuse, overlap, and architectural debt. The Market Analyst examines buyer behavior, substitutes, pricing evidence, procurement friction, and willingness-to-pay signals. The Policy Analyst examines regulation, public funding, mandates, standards, and institutional constraints. The Research Analyst evaluates feasibility and evidence maturity. The Participant Analyst tracks providers, suppliers, builders, buyers, users, communities, media, funders, and analysts as distinct actor classes. The Skeptic searches for disconfirming evidence and category errors. The Experiment Designer isolates the highest-risk assumption. The Governor enforces gates and asks for human authorization. No role may both propose and approve the same transition.

## Socratic descent

Every candidate opportunity is interrogated from surface claim to irreducible assumption.

The first question is what appears to be happening. The second is what evidence remains when promotional language is removed. The third is who is changing behavior rather than merely expressing interest. The fourth is what value is being exchanged, by whom, and under what constraint. The fifth is why existing products, institutions, or repositories do not already solve the problem. The sixth is what must remain true for the opportunity to persist for three years. The seventh is what observation would falsify the thesis. The eighth is which single assumption creates the greatest chance of failure. The ninth is the smallest ethical experiment that could test it. The final question is whether the work deserves durable infrastructure or only a temporary investigation.

A candidate cannot advance when an answer is replaced by rhetoric, an uncited assumption, or a proxy metric that does not demonstrate behavior.

## Reverse-engineering method

Comparable systems are studied as mechanism bundles rather than brands. Each comparable is decomposed into the problem recognized, actor served, triggering condition, value mechanism, distribution path, trust mechanism, operating model, evidence of traction, failure mode, and boundary.

At least three materially different comparables are required for a high-confidence thesis: one direct analogue, one adjacent-domain analogue, and one failed, stalled, criticized, or constrained analogue. Similar examples are not copied wholesale. Their mechanisms are compared, contradictions preserved, and only repeatedly supported mechanisms are synthesized.

The synthesis output states which mechanisms transfer, which do not, why they differ, and which assumptions remain untested.

## Opportunity convergence model

A repository thesis requires convergent evidence across independent dimensions: problem reality, behavioral traction, market capacity, policy or institutional relevance, research feasibility, ecosystem capability, differentiation, distribution access, economic sustainability, and reversibility of the first experiment.

Confidence is not a simple average. A fatal weakness in problem reality, legal viability, evidence integrity, or ecosystem fit blocks advancement regardless of strengths elsewhere. Promotional attention, social engagement, and media volume never count as independent evidence unless paired with behavior such as purchasing, procurement, adoption, funding, implementation, hiring, standards activity, or repeated participation.

Each dimension receives a score from zero to four. Zero means absent or contradicted. One means anecdotal. Two means plausible and source-backed. Three means independently corroborated. Four means demonstrated through behavior or repeated outcomes. A genesis candidate requires no fatal-gate failure, at least six dimensions at three or above, and an overall confidence of at least 0.82. A recommendation to run only a bounded experiment may proceed at 0.68 when the experiment is cheap, reversible, and tests the most dangerous assumption.

## Actor-behavior model

The weekly review tracks behavior by actor class instead of collapsing all activity into a single market signal.

Providers are examined for new offers, pricing, bundling, service guarantees, and category positioning. Suppliers are examined for capacity, lead times, component availability, tooling, standards support, and constraints. Builders are examined for releases, repositories, integrations, hiring, technical claims, and implementation velocity. Buyers are examined for procurement, pilots, budgets, renewals, requests, and switching behavior. Participants and communities are examined for retention, contribution, repeat use, governance activity, and unmet needs. Media is examined for narrative formation, repetition, skepticism, and category language. Funders are examined for grants, investments, thesis shifts, due-diligence criteria, and follow-on behavior. Analysts are examined for forecasts, benchmarks, standards, critiques, and changes in category definition.

Signals from different actor classes are retained separately so that attention cannot be mistaken for demand and funding cannot be mistaken for product-market fit.

## Core protocol

The operating protocol has nine stages.

Stage one collects evidence and records provenance. Stage two normalizes claims and separates event date from publication date. Stage three compares the evidence against prior runs and emits only material change. Stage four maps changes to actors, domains, repositories, and existing patterns. Stage five performs Socratic descent and identifies the irreducible assumptions. Stage six reverse-engineers comparables and performs adversarial contradiction search. Stage seven evaluates whether the opportunity belongs in an existing repository, a temporary experiment, a partnership, a service offer, a research program, or a new repository. Stage eight produces a strategy memorandum or repository thesis with explicit confidence, uncertainty, and kill conditions. Stage nine requests human authorization.

No stage may silently skip a missing predecessor. Every transition emits a receipt.

## Repository-fit decision

The default outcome is not a new repository.

An idea remains in an existing repository when its primary user, runtime, evidence model, governance, and roadmap remain substantially aligned. It becomes a bounded experiment when one decisive assumption is unresolved. It becomes a cross-repository initiative when the value depends on coordinated changes but no independent product identity is required. It becomes a new repository only when it has a distinct user or buyer, independent lifecycle, clear technical boundary, separate governance or licensing need, and a roadmap that would distort an existing repository.

The engine must explicitly document why the concept is not merely a feature, plugin, example, package, case study, service, or temporary branch.

## Repository thesis dossier

A genesis proposal contains the problem thesis, intended actors, evidence map, timing thesis, mechanism, system boundary, adjacent repositories, comparables, differentiation, policy and research context, distribution path, economic logic, first experiment, dangerous assumption, kill conditions, governance model, licensing posture, milestone plan, acceptance criteria, and unanswered questions.

The dossier must include both an advocate case and a skeptic case. It must state what evidence would increase confidence, what evidence would reduce confidence, and when the proposal expires if no confirming behavior appears.

## Gates

G0 is source integrity. Claims require retrievable provenance and date clarity.

G1 is observation integrity. Verified facts, inference, significance, and uncertainty must be separated.

G2 is behavioral evidence. At least two actor classes must show meaningful behavior, not only commentary.

G3 is comparative evidence. The direct, adjacent, and failed or constrained analogues must be analyzed.

G4 is ecosystem fit. Reuse, overlap, ownership, and boundary implications must be explicit.

G5 is adversarial survival. The skeptic must identify failure modes, substitutes, timing errors, and disconfirming evidence.

G6 is experimentability. The most dangerous assumption must be testable by a bounded, ethical, reversible experiment.

G7 is strategic sufficiency. Timing, benefit, opportunity cost, and posture must be explicit.

G8 is genesis sufficiency. The concept must pass the confidence threshold and prove that existing repositories are insufficient.

G9 is human authorization. Only the human governor may authorize repository creation, spending, partnership outreach, public claims, or implementation beyond a draft proposal.

## Boundaries

The engine may create candidate graph records, draft strategy memoranda, draft experiment plans, and draft repository dossiers. It may open draft pull requests in pre-approved paths when explicitly authorized. It may not create production repositories, contact external parties, submit grants, spend money, merge changes, accept knowledge, close issues, or publish market claims without approval.

It must not treat citation count as feasibility, social engagement as demand, funding as adoption, media coverage as durability, a single customer request as a category, or a technical prototype as a business.

## Benchmarks

Evidence traceability requires every strategic claim to resolve to source records and every derived recommendation to resolve to observations and patterns. Target: one hundred percent traceability.

Reproducibility requires repeated evaluation over unchanged inputs to produce the same classification, score, and receipt. Target: exact deterministic output for schema validation and scoring.

Calibration requires retrospective comparison between confidence and subsequent outcomes. Target: proposals scored above 0.82 should show materially higher validation rates than those between 0.68 and 0.81 after enough samples exist.

Restraint requires most reviewed signals not to become repository proposals. Initial target: fewer than five percent of candidate opportunities reach genesis review.

Novelty control requires every proposed repository to pass the existing-repository fit test and duplicate search. Target: zero proposals lacking an explicit non-feature justification.

Adversarial quality requires every thesis to include at least three credible failure modes and one disconfirming source or unresolved contradiction.

Operational latency requires the weekly synthesis to complete within its scheduled run window and emit a readable brief plus machine-readable records without blocking on noncritical collection failures.

Human attention requires the weekly brief to surface no more than three high-priority strategic recommendations and one genesis candidate unless an exceptional event is documented.

## Definition of done for v0

Version zero is complete when the schemas and protocol can ingest one week of research and repository evidence, classify actor behavior, perform the repository-fit decision, produce a scored strategy memorandum, produce or refuse a repository thesis, record all transitions, detect missing evidence and broken references, preserve contradictions, and emit a human-readable weekly review plus machine-readable output.

A successful v0 must demonstrate one positive case, one rejected case, and one deferred experiment case. It must prove that the system can say no for explicit reasons. It must not require a graph database, embedding service, or autonomous repository creation.

## Phased scope

The nearest horizon establishes repository-native schemas, deterministic scoring, Socratic questionnaires, comparable-analysis templates, weekly review output, and manual approval gates.

The next horizon adds graph traversal, longitudinal confidence calibration, actor-behavior trend analysis, duplicate detection across repositories, experiment-result ingestion, and generated draft dossiers.

The middle horizon adds interactive graph views, scenario comparison, portfolio-capacity constraints, opportunity-cost modeling, and integration with issue and pull-request workflows.

The long horizon becomes an evidence-calibrated strategic operating system that can identify emerging categories, propose new research and product infrastructure, simulate portfolio consequences, learn from accepted and rejected theses, and measure whether its recommendations created durable value. Human authority remains structurally preserved at every horizon.

## Weekly operating review

The weekly review compares the previous seven days with the prior four-week baseline. It reports material changes by actor class, strengthened and weakened patterns, contradictions, emerging category language, provider and supplier shifts, builder velocity, buyer behavior, participant retention or friction, media narrative movement, funder thesis changes, analyst benchmark changes, policy developments, and repository implications.

The review ends with at most three strategy recommendations, at most two experiments, and at most one repository-genesis candidate. It also names one attractive signal to ignore, one unresolved paradox, one missing evidence class, and one question that would materially alter the recommendation.

The weekly process emits a run receipt even when there is no material change. No-change is retained as evidence of stability rather than converted into filler.
